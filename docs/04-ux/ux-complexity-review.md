# UX Complexity and Cost Review — P04 UX Architecture

> Status: `PROPOSED — OWNER VALIDATION REQUIRED`. Provenance: `TECHNICAL_DISCOVERY`. This is a P04 UX architecture proposal, not an approved design and not a visual specification.
> Working assumptions: `WA-01`–`WA-05` (see `docs/04-ux/README.md`). `G-06` is UNSATISFIED.

This is a **specialist adversarial review that attacks UX features creating disproportionate cost** — including P04's own. It is not a feature backlog, not a prioritization exercise, not a roadmap, and not a defense of the P04 design. Its value is in what it removes. Every entry names what the feature would cost, what P04 does instead, and the specific condition under which the rejection should be revisited. Where P04 itself is the expensive party, this document says so and names the control rather than arguing the cost away.

**Cost is expressed on an ordinal scale — `NONE` · `LOW` · `MEDIUM` · `HIGH` · `VERY HIGH` — never as a figure.** P04 has no basis for developer-day, currency, or hour estimates: `SRC-006` is NOT RECEIVED, nothing has been implemented, and no usability or traffic evidence exists. Producing a number here would be fabrication dressed as rigour. Where a number is genuinely wanted, the row names the measurement that would produce it. The four cost dimensions are kept **separate** throughout, per `AGENTS.md`: infrastructure cash cost and human operational cost are different quantities and must never be summed into a headline.

---

## 1. Cost dimensions

| Dimension | What it measures | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Developer | One-time build plus the ongoing maintenance the surface creates for one part-time developer. | Delivery capacity. |
| Operator | Recurring human handling the feature generates — queues, moderation, disputes, support contacts, content maintenance. | Operating capacity. The modelled operator hours — 8.35 / 55.42 / 340.58 per month across the three scenarios — are **context for the size of the operating claim, not a budget any queue draws against**. They are **an illustrative sensitivity at an assumed USD $40/h against the modelled Pilot / Early Marketplace / Growth volumes; not a measurement, not derived from this queue set, and `SRC-006` is NOT RECEIVED.** Recomputed from `docs/03-technology/cost-model.md` §5.2, **roughly 142.5 of the 340.58 Growth hours — about 42% — belong to case classes with no P04 queue at all** (customer and provider support, 120 h; data-subject requests, 22.50 h). Treating the frame as a ceiling per queue is the derivation `docs/04-ux/operator-surfaces.md` §6 explicitly forbids. |
| Infrastructure | Recurring cash: compute, storage, egress, per-unit vendor charges, query load. | The cost model. |
| User friction | Steps, decisions, waiting, or confusion imposed on a customer or provider. Friction is a cost even when it is free to build. | Customers and providers. |

Disposition values: `REJECTED` (not V1, and the argument against it is structural) · `DEFERRED` (not V1, with a named reopening trigger) · `NARROWED` (a smaller thing ships in its place) · `ACCEPTED WITH CONTROL` (it ships, bounded by a stated control).

**These four are dispositions, not evidence labels**, even though they are backticked identically to the evidence vocabulary `AGENTS.md` fixes (`CONFIRMED`, `OBSERVED`, `PROPOSED`, `ASSUMPTION`, `FUTURE`, `REJECTED`) — and **`REJECTED` appears in both lists with two different meanings**. Here it is a disposition: what P04 recommends doing with a feature. An evidence label says how well a claim is supported. **The evidence status of every row in this document is `PROPOSED`**, whatever its disposition column says.

## 3. What P04 does instead, and why the substitute is not worse

| ID | The substitute | Why it is cheaper without being worse |
|---|---|---|
| `CX-01` | **Progressive, deterministic, category-governed intake.** Ask only the next question the selected `Category`'s `CategoryArchetype` requires, with every question justified against the five governed classes `DISCOVERY` / `PRE-SUBMIT` / `CATEGORY` / `QUALITY` / `NOT-V1`. | It is strictly less work for the customer, not a trimmed-down version of the same form: category and location come first, so the customer sees supply before investing. **A question that improves no decision and gates nothing is deleted.** Design target: a mobile performer request completes in **5 composer answers on the discovery path** (3 of them reused from discovery), up to 8 on direct arrival, and **10–11 total blocking items to submit** with identity counted — against a capture of 24 items, roughly twenty of which are presented as steps. **The earlier "6–9" figure was net of identity and is withdrawn** (`docs/04-ux/request-intake.md` §3). **This is a design target, not a measured improvement** — the measurement that would confirm it is per-step composer abandonment (`R-022`) compared against a baseline that does not yet exist. `SRC-006` is NOT RECEIVED. |
| `CX-02` | **`RequestIntake` (`accepting` / `paused` / `unconfirmed`) plus date as request context**, with feasibility determined by the provider inside the request interaction. | The customer still gets an answer about their date — from the provider, who is the only party that actually knows. The platform stops making a promise it cannot keep, and stops owning the failure when a calendar is stale. **"Not accepting requests" is not date unavailability and must never be worded as either.** |
| `CX-03` | **Coarse `Place` labels, a declared-coverage statement in words** ("Serves: Houston, Katy, Sugar Land"; "Travels up to 50 miles from Houston"), archetype-correct framing (venue = customer travels; performer = provider travels), units preserved as the provider entered them, and a **link out to the customer's own map application**. | It answers the customer's actual question — where is it, how far is it from me — without tiles, a vendor SDK, or an attribution clause. Note that **privacy is not the reason** for this decision; a city centroid emits nothing private. The reason is that no journey needs it. |

Removing presence removes a false obligation rather than a capability. The one thing genuinely lost is the reassurance that the other party is reading; that is replaced by **honest delivery state**, which is more informative and cannot be faked. |

| `CX-06` | **Structured category answers plus free-text notes inside the authenticated request surface.** | The structured answers carry the information an attachment would have carried, in a form the provider can actually filter and the platform can actually govern. Free text is **classified as possibly containing contact data regardless of policy**, and Superola must **not** tell users that "in-platform" means contact-protected (`ADR-010`). |
| `CX-07` | **The governed progressive composer, plus a governed synonym and alias table** for search — Unicode and diacritic folding (`norteño` → `norteno`), light stemming, exact/prefix/fuzzy matching against governed rows. | The synonym table is a **P04 prerequisite, not an AI substitute**, and it must be tried before any model is funded. P04 found **no interaction problem in the V1 surface set that assisted intake is the cheapest fix for**, and the progressive model already removes most of the friction assisted intake would target. |
| `CX-08` | **One deliberate recipient per `ServiceRequest`**, and for a second service on the same event: *Request another service for this event* seeds a **new `RequestDraft` pre-filled from the previous request's `EventContext`**, carrying the customer-declared `eventGroupingHint`. | It is the same aggregate used twice — **no new entity, no routing engine, no consent model**. | `CX-09` | **Governed `CategoryAttributeDefinition`s rendered by one composer, one profile surface, and one results surface.** Provider category input becomes a `CategoryProposal`, never a node. | The marginal cost of a Category falls to governance data entry. Transportation is modelled and **recommended out of the launch cohort**, because its route-corridor semantics do not reduce to a containment predicate. |

## 4. Where P04 itself is the expensive party

The rows above attack other people's proposals. These four attack P04's. Each is genuinely expensive, and the honest position is stated rather than the cost argued away.

| ID | P04's own expensive decision | The honest cost | Why it is still the right call — and where the argument is weakest | Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CX-19` | **Eight case queues at launch (`OQ-02`–`OQ-09`), plus one index surface (`UX-26`)** — nine surfaces, `UX-26`–`UX-34`, with `UX-29` carrying two case kinds. | **This is the largest single operator-cost commitment in P04, and it is unmeasured.** Eight case queues at launch is already a workload claim: eight backlogs to watch, eight sets of handling conventions to write, eight ways to fall behind — plus a ninth surface to build that creates no cases of its own. **An index is not a queue**, and the workload count is eight. The modelled operator hours — 8.35 / 55.42 / 340.58 per month across the three scenarios — are context, and they are **an illustrative sensitivity at an assumed USD $40/h against the modelled Pilot / Early Marketplace / Growth volumes; not a measurement, not derived from this queue set, and `SRC-006` is NOT RECEIVED.** At the top scenario 340.58 hours per month is **the size of the whole modelled operating claim** — the equivalent of a job's worth of case work across all case classes — **and it is not a measurement of these queues**: roughly **142.5 of those hours, about 42%, fall in case classes with no P04 queue at all** (customer and provider support, data-subject requests). `docs/04-ux/operator-surfaces.md` §6 states in bold that no figure there may be quoted as a measurement of this queue set. | The weak point in the argument is the **count**, not the principle.

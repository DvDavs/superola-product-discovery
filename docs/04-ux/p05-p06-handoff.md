# P05 and P06 Handoff — P04 UX Architecture

> Status: `PROPOSED — OWNER VALIDATION REQUIRED`. Provenance: `TECHNICAL_DISCOVERY`. This is a P04 UX architecture proposal, not an approved design and not a visual specification.
> Working assumptions: `WA-01`–`WA-05` (see `docs/04-ux/README.md`). `G-06` is UNSATISFIED.

This document identifies the inputs that P04's UX architecture creates for two later phases — P05 (Legacy Audit and Migration) and P06 (MVP and Roadmap). **It does not start, design, or authorize either phase.** It contains no migration design, no migration recommendation, no cohort proposal, no roadmap, no sequencing, no estimate, and no commitment. It is a list of things a later phase will have to decide, with the P04 constraint that will still be binding when it does.

**P04 does not design the legacy migration.** `docs/06-migration/legacy-data-strategy.md` is NOT STARTED, P05 owns the migration strategy (`ADR-009`), and the owner plus qualified counsel own lawful basis (`G-09`, `OR-009`) — a fact David cannot supply. **If no cohort is approved, the legacy boundary is empty and the claim queue is zero.** Every row in §1 is therefore conditional, and none of them is work P04 is asking anyone to do.

**P04 produces no developer-day, currency, or effort figures of its own anywhere, including in §2 — with exactly one exception, named here rather than discovered later.** §2 reproduces the modelled operator-hours frame from `docs/03-technology/cost-model.md` §5.2 (8.35 / 55.42 / 340.58 hours per month), because P06 cannot size an operator team without it. **It is reproduced, never computed, and it is not P04's figure**; it is an illustrative sensitivity at an assumed USD $40/h against the modelled volumes, it was **not derived from P04's queue set**, and roughly 42% of it at the Growth scenario belongs to case classes with no P04 queue at all. It has no basis for them: nothing has been implemented, no usability or traffic evidence exists, and `SRC-006` is NOT RECEIVED. §2 supplies **counts and classifications only**. Converting a count into an estimate is P06's act, performed with P06's evidence, and P04 does not pre-empt it.

## 1. P05 — Legacy Audit and Migration: UX impacts only

Scope of this section: **the surfaces a human would see if a legacy cohort were approved**, and the P04 constraints that would still hold. Not in scope: what to migrate, whether to migrate, import mechanics, provenance modelling, batch reversibility, suppression, taxonomy mapping, media-rights review, counts, yields, or lawful basis. All of those are P05's, `ADR-009`'s, or counsel's.

| ID | UX input P05 inherits | Why it is a UX question and not only a data question | Binding P04 or P02 constraint | What P04 does not decide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `H5-01` | **Existing-account claim surface** (`UX-36`, `FUTURE` / conditional). The surface a person reaches when they believe a `LegacyProviderRecord` describes their business. | It is the only surface in the product where a stranger asserts ownership of data about someone else, so its information design *is* its security design. | **Disclose nothing before a claim grant.** **Even a masked contact hint is an enumeration oracle** (`ADR-009`), so partial reveals — `j•••@gmail.com`, a masked phone suffix, a "we found a record matching that email" confirmation, a differing error message, or a differing response time between found and not-found — are all prohibited. The surface must behave identically whether or not a record exists. | The evidence bar for granting a claim, who bears the residual risk of a wrong grant, and the reversal committed to the losing party (`Q-025`, `OWNER` + legal/privacy + operations). P04 does not set it and does not guess it. |
| `H5-02` | **Claim outcome states as the claimant experiences them**: submitted, under review, granted, denied, and denied-after-dispute. | A denial with no explanation is the worst outcome available: the claimant cannot tell whether they were rejected, whether the record is someone else's, or whether it exists. A denial that explains too much is `H5-01`'s oracle by another route. | The claimant sees a **policy-level outcome only**, in text (never color alone), with no disclosure of the record's contents, no disclosure of a competing claimant, and no disclosure of what evidence failed. Same treatment as the block/report surface: a policy-level reason and nothing more. | Whether a denied claim may be re-attempted, after how long, and with what additional evidence. That is P05 policy plus `Q-025`. |
| `H5-03` | **Profile migration and reactivation** — what a granted claim actually produces for the provider. | Providers will expect a claim to restore a working profile. It does not, and the surface must set that expectation before the claim, not after. | **A claim binds ownership and does not publish.** It seeds a **new `ProviderProfile` in `Draft`** through the same governed commands a fresh provider uses (`ADR-009`). The claimant therefore lands in `UX-17`/`UX-24` with a live requirements checklist and completes ordinary onboarding. **`publicationGateMet` is not weakened for imported data** — see `H5-05`. | Which fields, if any, may pre-fill the `Draft` from the legacy record, and under which lawful basis. That is P05 plus counsel, per-field, per `ADR-009`'s per-field provenance requirement. |
| `H5-04` | **Forced credential reset**, and what the user sees when one is required. | If legacy credentials cannot be trusted, "sign in" is the wrong door and must not be offered, because a failed sign-in against a legacy identity is itself an enumeration signal. | Authentication is application-owned (`ADR-017`) and there is no runtime coupling to the legacy platform in either direction (`ADR-009`), so a legacy credential **cannot** be verified at runtime by construction. The V1-honest position follows: the user establishes an `Account` on the new platform and proves control of a contact channel, exactly as a fresh provider does. The `verificationBasis` remains **control of this contact channel was proven at time T** and nothing more. The reset surface must not imply that the old password was checked, was wrong, or existed. **A reset notification body stays inside the `ADR-010` allowlist.** | Whether legacy accounts are communicated to at all, through which channel, in which locale, and under which lawful basis (`G-09`, `Q-029`, `Q-030`). P04 does not decide and does not draft the message. |
| `H5-05` | **Incomplete imported profiles versus `publicationGateMet`.** | The predictable pressure in any migration is to publish imported supply quickly to solve cold start. The gate is what stops that, and the UX is where the pressure lands. | **`publicationGateMet` is archetype-aware and is not relaxed for imported records.** All governed required attributes at the current requirement version; at least one `VerificationFact` proving control of a provider contact channel; media rights acknowledged for every referenced asset; at least one `ServiceOffering` with an archetype-valid `ServiceAreaDeclaration` and a `BaseLocation` of sufficient coordinate precision; no blocking moderation state; entitlement eligible. Imported records pass **the same publication state machine** fresh providers pass. **A `LegacyProviderRecord` is structurally incapable of appearing in discovery or receiving a request** — that is a structural property, not a flag, and no UX may route around it. **Silent publication of an incomplete profile must not happen.** | Whether an imported cohort is approved at all, and what proportion could realistically satisfy the gate. That denominator is the whole migration risk and it is P05's to measure, not P04's to assume. |
| `H5-06` | **Stale providers** — imported supply that reaches `Published` and then decays, and imported supply that is stale on arrival. | Legacy records are stale by construction; a migration that succeeds technically can still make the marketplace look broken, which is the highest-consequence migration risk (`R-016`). Customers see that as product failure, not data failure. | `Stale` **stays discoverable and flagged, never hidden**, and carries a freshness indicator in text (never color alone). **Customers must not be able to distinguish `Suspended` from `Deactivated`.** `Stale` → `reconfirm` is a **one-action path from a notification link**, completable on a phone — the highest-frequency provider maintenance action in the product, and the single most load-bearing UX element in any reactivation attempt. `UX-33` is the operator queue for staleness and `RequestIntake` decay. | The staleness threshold, the `RequestIntake` decay window, and the reactivation campaign design. The decay window is explicitly outside P04's decisions. |
| `H5-07` | **Old-URL redirect and canonical-URL ownership during coexistence** (`Q-019`). | This is the UX impact most likely to be discovered too late. Every provider profile is a public URL; a coexistence period without a settled owner splits provider identity across two systems and forfeits accumulated search authority (`R-028`). It is also a **user-visible correctness problem**: a customer following an old link must arrive somewhere that is true, not at a profile Superola has not been authorized to publish. | Coexistence is **edge-level routing and URL-identity ownership only — no runtime coupling in either direction** (`ADR-009`). **A redirect must never resolve to a record that has not passed `publicationGateMet`**, because a redirect into an unpublished or non-existent profile is both a broken journey and, if it discloses anything about the record, an `H5-01` oracle. Public distribution and crawler policy remain an approval gate; `Q-027` (deletion commitment on surfaces Superola does not control) **must be settled before `Q-015`**. | **`Q-019` is OPEN**: which system owns provider identity and canonical public URLs, who holds redirect authority, and what triggers cutover or rollback. Owner + David + P05 + a public-content baseline. **Cutover requires this settled first** (`ADR-009`). P04 states the constraint and does not answer the question. |
| `H5-08` | **Coexistence of legacy and new supply without a shared publication surface.** | The tempting UX shortcut is one results page that blends both, so the marketplace looks full on day one. That shortcut is the thing `ADR-009` exists to make impossible. | **There is no shared publication surface and there must not be one.** Discovery, eligibility, and request delivery operate exclusively on `Published`/`Stale` `ProviderProfile`s through `EligibilityDecision`'s six named inputs. A `LegacyProviderRecord` is a different kind of thing and cannot enter that path. Any surface that renders both classes together — a results page, a category browse page, a `Market` page, a count, or a "providers in your area" figure — reintroduces the **accidental-publication path from unverified imported data straight into the public marketplace** that `ADR-009` Option B was rejected for. **A cold-start supply count that includes unclaimed legacy records is the same defect wearing a metric's clothes.** | Whether coexistence happens at all, for how long, and what the cutover trigger is. P05 plus `Q-019`. |
| `H5-09` | **Operator claim and duplicate queue** (`UX-29`), and the audited-access surface it depends on (`UX-34`). | Claim adjudication, duplicate resolution, and reachability verification are **manual exception queues** (`ADR-009`). If broad migration were approved, the operator queue would become the dominant cost — which makes this a capacity question before it is a design question. | Each queue records reason, state, priority where justified, actor or owner, and an auditable action. **Every private-content access is audited and reason-tagged with a recorded operational purpose** (`Q-026` auditability limb, resolved). **The claim limb of `UX-29` is empty unless a legacy cohort is approved** — plainly: **if no cohort is approved, the claim queue is zero and this surface has no launch workload.** | Queue volume, adjudication time, staffing, and whether operating capacity exists without breaching a backlog-age stop rule. P05 measures; P06 plans. See `docs/04-ux/ux-complexity-review.md` `CX-19`. |
| `H5-10` | **Locale of any legacy-facing surface or communication.** | Legacy records carry no reliable locale and jurisdiction is frequently unknown for them (`A-025`). A claim invitation or reset notice sent in the wrong language is a reachability failure that will be recorded as a low claim yield — attributing a UX defect to the data. | `contentLocale` is explicit; **machine-translated content is explicitly marked** so no surface presents a translation as the provider's own words. No fixed-width control may depend on English string length. `unknown` jurisdiction maps to the **strictest** handling among the working markets. | **`Q-020` is OPEN** — canonical locale for a profile and for a request conversation, and who authors additional locales. P04 records a recommendation in `docs/04-ux/accessibility-and-responsive.md` §8.1 and does **not** claim it resolved. |

### 1.1 What P04 hands P05, stated plainly

- P04 **does not design the migration**, does not recommend a cohort, does not estimate yield, and does not opine on lawful basis.
- `UX-36` is `FUTURE` / conditional in `docs/04-ux/surface-inventory.md` and is **not in the V1 slice**. It is inventoried so that its constraints are recorded before anyone designs it, not because P04 is asking for it.
- **If no cohort is approved, `H5-01` through `H5-05`, `H5-09`, and `H5-10` are empty**, `UX-36` is never built, and the claim limb of `UX-29` has zero volume. `H5-06` (staleness) and `H5-07`/`H5-08` (URL ownership and non-blending) remain live regardless, because staleness applies to freshly onboarded supply and URL ownership applies to any coexistence at all.
- The one thing P04 asks P05 to carry unchanged: **the enumeration-oracle rule in `H5-01`**. It is cheap to honour at design time and structurally unrecoverable if violated once.
